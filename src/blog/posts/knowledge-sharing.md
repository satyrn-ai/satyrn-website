Knowledge sharing brings people together. Why do I think this matters? I believe it benefits the people who learn, the groups they belong to, and the people doing the sharing.

<!-- more -->

## Expansion

While most resources shrink when you share them, knowledge, however, grows and
compounds when distributed. If you explain something to five other people,
you still know it, and you've invited five others into the mix, so now six
people can build on it, improve it, and pass it on. Progress often comes
from building on top of what others have already done, rather than
starting from scratch.

Sharing also deepens the knowledge itself. Feedback, fixes, other
opinions, and perspectives all test the ideas and help them grow.

## Access

Distributed learning widens access and opportunity. Too often,
getting to learn something can be decided by luck. You can be helped or hindered by where
you live, what you can afford, how you grew up, for example. When knowledge
is shared openly, more people get the chance to learn, and the gap between
those who have access and those who don't starts to shrink. If you have a
laptop and a good tutorial, you can access knowledge previously kept behind
university walls. Learning online also means you can go at your own pace and
in your own way, and free material removes the cost of entry.

None of this closes the gap on its own. An internet connection, a device, time,
and the language the material is written in all still matter. That's a good
reason to treat accessibility as something we keep working on, by explaining
patiently, sharing freely, and welcoming the people who show up.

## Resilience

Knowledge sharing builds resilience. How many times have you been part of a team where all the
knowledge for one specific thing lives inside the brain of one person? Then that
person inevitably goes on a long holiday or leaves the team, and all that
knowledge goes with them. When knowledge is shared, no one person has to carry
it alone, and a group can more readily transition through change, with people
coming and going. The team with an irreplaceable person is fragile; the team where knowledge is spread is stronger.

## Understanding

Knowledge sharing helps the person doing the sharing, too. You don't really know something until you
have to explain it to someone else. If you have to shape something in a way
that teaches it to someone else, you deepen your own understanding, and build
self-confidence.

It's also why learners make good teachers. When you have just tested something out you're
at the exact right moment to help out someone else. You've been through all
the messy bits the expert has often forgotten.

## Community

When it comes to community, when people share what they know, they are building
trust. When there's trust, people feel more confident to ask questions, give
feedback, admit what they don't know. People who share and receive knowledge
form relationships. Conversations matter as a sharing tool here too –
being able to start a thread, ask "hey, how do you do this?", and being met with
someone willing to help – that can enrich what you can get out
of a tutorial or an article. These conversations are also where people get to
know one another, and where they continue to build that connection that makes them
more willing to share again, and so the loop of sharing and community keeps
turning.

## Vulnerability

Sharing takes effort and vulnerability. It can take time, and it can be hard.
Many of us deal with that little inner voice that says we're not "expert"
enough, that someone else could explain it better. But that little voice is
wrong. Everyone has something to say, something to share. Each time you share,
it gets a little easier. It also makes it easier for the next person to do the same.
